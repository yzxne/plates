## Why this order

Big compound lifts first, isolation and small muscles last. Evidence: hypertrophy is similar whichever order (Nunes et al. 2021, *Eur J Sport Sci* meta-analysis), but lifts done earlier get stronger and get more reps (Simão group), so priority lifts go first. Push/pull supersets keep volume and save time (Weakley et al. meta-analysis); same-muscle supersets cut volume. Rules of thumb kept in the coach instructions: if a muscle lags, move its first exercise earlier; on Upper, dips come after the shoulder press (swap if dips become a priority).

## Athletic Legs: why

For knee/back durability and mobility. Strength training through full range improves range of motion about as much as stretching (Afonso et al. 2021, 11 studies). Strength training cut sports injuries to about a third and balance training by ~45 %, stretching showed no effect (Lauersen et al. 2014). Programs including Nordic curls roughly halved hamstring injuries (van Dyk et al. 2019; a later reanalysis called it inconclusive). The Copenhagen adduction program lowered groin problems by 41 % in football players (Harøy et al. 2019).

## Warm-up and cool-down

Built into the workout screen (`shared/warmups.ts`, details in algorithm.md → Warm-ups): easy cardio + 3–4 dynamic moves at the start, ramp-up chips on the first big lift (50/70/85 % of working weight), one light ramp set for a later big lift, stretches at the end.

## Logging conventions

- **Dumbbells:** weight = both dumbbells combined (two 40s = 80). Old Hevy sets logged per dumbbell were doubled on 2026-11-18.
- **Gym change on 2026-08-01:** Settings → *Lift records start* = 2026-08-01. Records, PR pop-ups, stalls and bests ignore machine/cable/barbell sets before it; dumbbell lifts keep all history.
- Unilateral lifts (split squats, single-leg RDL, lunges): reps are per leg.
